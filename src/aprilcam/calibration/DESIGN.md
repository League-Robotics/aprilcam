# calibration/

**Owner:** Eric Busboom · **Last reviewed:** 2026-08-21 · **Status:** stable

---

## 1. Purpose

`calibration/` establishes and holds one camera's pixel↔world mapping — the sole owner of the conversion boundary that `docs/design/design.md` principle 5a calls "world coordinates first; pixels only at the edges." Nothing else in the codebase is permitted to solve a homography, undistort a lens point, or perform the deskew warp: this subsystem is where a raw camera pixel and the field's own A1-centred centimetre coordinate system are proven to correspond, and where that proof is stored so later frames can be converted without re-detecting anything.

Most of it is daemon-only, per the single-vision-authority principle (`design.md` §2.1): `calibration.py`, `distortion.py`, and `deskew.py` import `cv2` at module scope. `interfaces.py` and — **as of sprint 036** — `geometry.py` are `cv2`-free; this is what lets `client/renderer.py` and `cli/locate.py` (outside this package) import `geometry.py`'s pure point-conversion/deskew-geometry/mount-correction functions without pulling `cv2` into a base-only install, and what lets the cv2-free client-side packages (`client/`, `view/`, `mcp/`, mechanically enforced by `tests/test_no_cv2_in_client_modules.py`) hold `Calibration`/`CalibrationResult` as pure value types without a vision dependency.

The subsystem has a real, substantial implementation — `calibration.py` alone is ~1470 lines, `geometry.py` ~660, `distortion.py` ~90, `deskew.py` ~180, `flatfield.py` ~250 (sprint 037) — covering multi-frame averaged ArUco/AprilTag detection, all-or-nothing id matching against a playfield's surveyed ground truth, degenerate-geometry rejection, an all-or-nothing homography solve with reprojection-error gating, optional lens-distortion modelling and intrinsics estimation (`cv2.calibrateCamera`), an opportunistic drift check, mount/parallax correction math, a camera-geometry solver, fixed-bounding-box deskew warp geometry, and — sprint 037 — flat-field gain-map construction and quality-gating. (Any planning document that still describes this package as "interface-only" predates that work and is stale.)

**Sprint 037's addition and the cohesion tension it creates, named rather than hidden.** This package's purpose sentence above is "establishes and holds one camera's pixel↔world mapping" — a flat-field gain map is a *radiometric* (per-pixel brightness/colour) correction, not a geometric one, so it does not literally fit that sentence. It lives here anyway, as a second, closely-related sibling responsibility ("measured artifacts tied to one camera's current mounting and lighting, persisted next to each other, reloaded at daemon startup the same way"), not folded into the same sentence. See Design Rationale below ("Home for flat-field capture math") for the alternatives considered and why this package won over a new top-level subsystem or `daemon/` itself.

## 2. Orientation

Five files, one `__init__.py` with no re-exports — every consumer imports from the specific submodule it needs.

- **`interfaces.py`** — the abstract contract, following the project's no-`Protocol` rule (CLAUDE.md): `CalibrationResult` and `Calibration` are frozen `@dataclass(kw_only=True)` value types; `Calibrator` and `Deskewer` are plain base classes whose methods raise `NotImplementedError`. `cv2`-free by design, so it is safe for cv2-free packages to import.
- **`calibration.py`** — the engine. Concrete `Calibration` (adds `homography`, `matched_ids`, `reprojection_error_cm`, and optional `camera_matrix`/`dist_coeffs`) and concrete `Calibrator` (adds `.calibrate()` and the additive `.get_calibration()`). Owns id matching, the homography solve, degenerate-geometry rejection, lens-distortion handling, and intrinsics estimation (`_estimate_intrinsics`, `_sanity_check_intrinsics`). Also hosts the module-level `compute_drift_cm` opportunistic-drift function. Imports `cv2` directly (its own tag-detection pipeline, `cv2.findHomography`, `cv2.calibrateCamera`) and calls into `distortion.py` for point-level undistort/redistort.
- **`geometry.py`** — pure NumPy point math, **`cv2`-free as of sprint 036** (previously imported `cv2` solely for the two functions that moved to `distortion.py`, below — nothing that remains here ever called `cv2`). No I/O, no detector objects, no camera access, no homography *solving*. Three families of functions live here: (1) `apply_homography`/`invert_homography`/`pixel_to_world`/`world_to_pixel`, the point-conversion half behind `Calibration.to_world`/`to_pixel`; (2) `deskewed_output_size`/`world_to_deskewed_pixel`, the deskew-geometry math `deskew.py` and `client/renderer.py` both build on; (3) `project_tag_to_field_plane`/`apply_mount_offset`/`correct_tag_pose` (UC-12 mount correction) and `solve_camera_geometry`/`ReferenceObservation`/`CameraGeometrySolution` (the inverse camera-geometry solver behind `aprilcam camera locate`).
- **`distortion.py`** (new, sprint 036) — `undistort_point`/`distort_point`, the point-level lens-distortion round trip, and their two tuning constants (`_DISTORT_POINT_MAX_ITERATIONS`, `_DISTORT_POINT_TOLERANCE_PX`). The only genuinely `cv2`-dependent point-level math in this package (`cv2.undistortPoints`), split out of `geometry.py` specifically so `geometry.py` itself could become `cv2`-free — see §4's "Rejected alternatives" for the reasoning. Consumed only by `calibration.py`; no client-side or `cli/` consumer needs lens distortion directly.
- **`deskew.py`** — `Deskewer`, the one class in the package that touches OpenCV's warp machinery (`cv2.getPerspectiveTransform`/`cv2.warpPerspective`). Constructed from an already-solved `Calibration` plus a playfield's `width_cm`/`height_cm`; delegates all its geometry decisions to `geometry.py`'s pure functions and does only the mechanical work they can't do — reading four pixels of raw camera content via `Calibration.to_pixel` and calling into OpenCV.
- **`flatfield.py`** (new, sprint 037) — `FlatFieldCapturer`, mirroring `Calibrator`'s shape closely: constructed with an injected frame source (never opens a camera itself), builds a per-channel gain map from a captured frame burst plus a field-interior mask, and quality-gates the result. Reuses `deskew.py`'s `_world_corners` (via a small shared helper) to turn a `Calibration` plus a playfield's `width_cm`/`height_cm` into pixel corners for the calibrated-mask path, and `vision.detector.TagDetector` directly for the uncalibrated ArUco-corner-detection fallback. The only module in this package besides `calibration.py` that runs a detector.

Relationship: `calibration.py` is the only thing that *produces* a `Calibration`; it consumes both `geometry.py` (homography solve support, deskew/mount math) and `distortion.py` (undistort/distort). `geometry.py` is pure math consumed by `calibration.py` and `deskew.py` (deskew sizing/anchor math) — and, deliberately, directly by two things outside the package: `client/renderer.py` (`world_to_deskewed_pixel`, for placing annotations on a deskewed image from published metadata alone, no `Calibration` object) and `cli/locate.py` (`solve_camera_geometry`, the one client-side vision-adjacent computation in the whole `cli/` package, justified in that module's own docstring as "pure algebra over already-vision-processed `WorldPoint`s"). Neither of those two outside consumers imports `distortion.py`, and neither needs to. `deskew.py` depends on `calibration.py` only for the `Calibration` type it's constructed with.

## 3. Constraints and Invariants

- **World frame is sacrosanct** (`design.md` §6 / `specification.md` §7): A1-centred, +x east, +y north, cm units; origin shift is a pure subtraction on both axes, never a y-flip. `geometry.py`'s docstring states this module contains **no origin-shift code at all, deliberately** — the homography `calibration.py` solves maps pixels directly onto a `PlayfieldDefinition`'s real, already-A1-centred surveyed positions, so there is no intermediate corner-relative coordinate system to get the shift wrong on. Violate this and the field silently mirrors north/south while every number still looks plausible.
- **Deskewed image: north up, east right; y-flip exactly once.** `world_to_deskewed_pixel` is the single place that flips y (image rows grow downward); `deskew.py` and `client/renderer.py` both call it rather than re-deriving the sign, so the sign only has to be right in one place. Violate this (e.g. by re-deriving the flip in a client) and the two paths can silently disagree.
- **All-or-nothing acceptance, no partial calibration** (UC-04). `CalibrationResult.succeeded` is binary: either every id `PlayfieldDefinition.required_aruco_ids()` names was found, a homography was solved, and its reprojection error is under threshold — or nothing is stored and any previously-good `Calibration` is left untouched. Reporting "9 of 10 found" as any kind of success would leave a camera silently serving a wrong mapping.
- **Completeness is necessary but not sufficient** (ticket 023-006, resolved). A complete-but-sloppy solve is still rejected: `MAX_REPROJECTION_ERROR_CM = 1.0` cm (stakeholder-confirmed — ~5x headroom over the observed-good 0.179 cm live solve, ~0.7% of `main-playfield`'s 134.3 cm width). Skipping this check would accept a numerically valid but practically useless homography.
- **Degenerate geometry is rejected before OpenCV ever sees it.** `_degeneracy_reason` checks point count (<4) and collinearity (via SVD ratio, `_COLLINEARITY_RATIO = 1e-3`) on both the pixel and world point sets *before* calling `cv2.findHomography`, because that function does not reliably refuse a collinear correspondence set — it can return a numerically well-formed matrix that is not a trustworthy 2D homography ("valid-but-bad", the exact failure UC-04 names). Skipping this pre-check would let a degenerate solve through silently.
- **Undistort once, feed one array everywhere** (Design Rationale Decision 1, sprint 030 ticket 001). When a camera's lens model is known, every detected marker pixel is undistorted *before* it reaches both the homography solve and the reprojection-error measurement — the same `pixel_pts` array feeds both. This directly encodes a real historical bug: v1 (`archive/.../calibration.py:1342-1346`) solved the homography from undistorted points but measured RMS error against a *different*, still-distorted point set, silently reporting a healthy error for a solve that was not. There is no separate "undistorted pixel_pts" variable in the current code specifically to prevent this recurring.
- **Camera frame access is handed in, never owned.** `Calibrator` is constructed with a `capture_frames` callable, not a camera handle — it never opens a camera. This is what makes `NoPlayfieldLinked`'s "raised before any frame is captured" contract mechanically true (not just documented): the playfield-link check runs against the injected `get_camera_config`/`get_playfield` callables *before* `capture_frames` is ever called, so an unlinked camera causes zero calls into whatever backend `capture_frames` wraps (UC-15.S-2's acceptance criterion). Violating this boundary (e.g. Calibrator opening its own camera) would silently capture from unconfigured hardware and couple this pure-logic module to a concrete camera backend.
- **Homography/intrinsics feature split.** An elevated feature (`height_cm > 0`) does not lie on the field plane; feeding it into the homography solve corrupts the solve exactly like unmodelled parallax. So the homography solve and `reprojection_error_cm` use **only** the coplanar (`height_cm == 0`) ArUco subset of matched ids — never AprilTags, never an elevated ArUco entry. Intrinsics estimation, by contrast, uses **every** surveyed-and-detected point of either family, coplanar and elevated alike, with real 3D object points `(x, y, height_cm)`. Mixing these up would corrupt the homography with parallax error, or starve intrinsics estimation of the 3D structure it needs.
- **Calibration is robot-agnostic** (`design.md` principle 5). `Calibration`/`CalibrationResult` never carry robot-specific facts (tag mount height, mount offset/yaw) — those are runtime mobile-tag registrations applied by the daemon as post-processing. The UC-12 mount-correction functions in `geometry.py` take camera geometry and mount parameters as plain function arguments; nothing is stored on `Calibration` itself.
- **Persistence is not this package's job.** `Calibration.to_dict`/`from_dict` define the JSON *shape* only; the atomic file write (`calibration.json` before `config.json`, per the global write-ordering convention) and startup reload belong to `daemon/service.py`. `calibration/` touches no filesystem. **Sprint 037 extends this unchanged**: `FlatFieldResult`/`FlatFieldInfo` are plain return values — `flatfield.py` never writes `flatfield.npy`/`flatfield.json` itself; `daemon/service.py` does, the same division `Calibrator`/`calibrate` already established.
- **Flat-field capture never opens a camera** (sprint 037, mirrors the "camera frame access is handed in, never owned" invariant above exactly). `FlatFieldCapturer.build` takes an already-captured frame burst (`Sequence[np.ndarray]`) and a precomputed boolean field-interior mask as arguments — it never touches `Camera`, `CameraConfig`, or any exposure setting. Pushing/restoring the temporary exposure override and deriving the mask (from `Calibration` or ArUco detection) are both `daemon/service.py`'s job, exactly like frame capture is for `Calibrator`.
- **The quality gate never mutates a previously-good map on refusal** (sprint 037, mirrors the "all-or-nothing acceptance" calibration invariant above). `FlatFieldResult.succeeded=False` — in-field clipping, implausible (>10x) spread, or frame-to-frame disagreement beyond the expected flicker tolerance — means `flatfield.py` returns without writing anything; `daemon/service.py`'s `capture_flat_field` then makes no filesystem write and leaves any existing `flatfield.npy`/`flatfield.json` and the live-applied map on the held-open `Camera` completely untouched, unless the caller passed `force=True`.
- **"Cannot verify" is a distinct outcome from "confirmed no drift."** `compute_drift_cm` returns `None` — never `0.0` — when fewer than `MIN_DRIFT_CHECK_MARKERS` (3) of a calibration's matched markers are visible in a later frame (darkness, occlusion, a covered field). Collapsing "cannot verify" into either a false-stale or a false-confirmed reading would violate UC-11.S-3: an advisory a caller can't trust is worse than none.

## 4. Design

**Data structures and ownership.** `CalibrationResult` (frozen dataclass) is the outcome of one `calibrate()` attempt: `succeeded`, `matched_ids`/`missing_ids` (named by id, not count, so a developer knows which marker to uncover), `reprojection_error_cm`, `reason` (free text for a complete-but-degenerate or complete-but-sloppy rejection), and the sprint-030 intrinsics-reporting triple `intrinsics_estimated`/`intrinsics_mode`/`elevated_features_used`. It never carries the fitted numbers themselves — an operator is told *whether* something changed, never the internal matrix, mirroring `get_calibration`'s existing stance. `Calibration` (frozen dataclass) is the stored, file-backed mapping: `stale`, `calibrated_at`, `playfield` (name only, not the object — replacing the definition is what marks a calibration stale), `homography` (a plain `(3,3)` nested tuple of floats, not an `ndarray`, because a numpy field would break the dataclass's auto-generated `__eq__`), `matched_ids`, `reprojection_error_cm`, and optional `camera_matrix`/`dist_coeffs` (also plain tuples, same reason). `Calibrator` holds an internal `Dict[str, Calibration]` behind a `threading.Lock`, plus one reused `TagDetector` (building an `ArucoDetector` is not free) and every injected callable (`get_camera_config`, `get_playfield`, `capture_frames`, `detector_factory`, `get_prior_calibration`).

**The calibration algorithm, end to end** (`Calibrator.calibrate`):
1. Resolve `camera_slug` -> `CameraConfig` -> linked `PlayfieldDefinition`; raise `NoPlayfieldLinked` if either is missing, before any frame capture. Raise `TypeError` if the definition isn't the concrete `config.loader.PlayfieldDefinition` (defensive — every real definition is built by `ConfigLoader.load_playfield` or `daemon/rpc.py`, so this guards a hypothetical future subclass).
2. If `estimate_intrinsics=True`, check the playfield surveys ≥ `MIN_SURVEYED_INTRINSICS_POINTS` (6) ArUco+AprilTag points — checked before any frame, since nothing a frame could show changes this answer.
3. Capture frames via the injected callable; raise `RuntimeError` if none returned.
4. `_detect_averaged` runs every frame through `vision.pipeline.run_pipeline` then the shared `TagDetector`, averaging each `(family, id)`'s detected pixel centre across whichever frames it appeared in — both ArUco and AprilTag families are kept (AprilTags exist purely for intrinsics estimation; they were discarded here before sprint 030 ticket 002).
5. All-or-nothing match: `matched_ids`/`missing_ids` against `required_aruco_ids()`. Any stray detected id not in the definition is logged and ignored, never matched. Any missing id fails the whole attempt immediately.
6. `homography_ids` = matched ids whose surveyed `height_cm == 0.0` — the coplanar/elevated split described above.
7. Intrinsics: if `estimate_intrinsics=True`, builds `(x, y, height_cm)` object points and matching raw (never undistorted) image points from every matched ArUco entry plus every surveyed-and-detected AprilTag entry, then calls `_estimate_intrinsics` (below). A rejected fit fails the whole `calibrate()` call. If `False`, carries forward whatever lens model the camera's *prior successful* calibration held, read via `get_prior_calibration` rather than `get_calibration` directly (see the restart-carry-forward bug note below).
8. If intrinsics are known (freshly estimated or carried forward), every `homography_ids` pixel is undistorted via `geometry.undistort_point`, producing the one `pixel_pts` array that feeds both the solve and the error measurement (the v1-lesson invariant above).
9. `_degeneracy_reason` checks point count and collinearity on both point sets; a hit returns a named failure.
10. `_solve_homography` calls `cv2.findHomography(..., method=0)` — regular least squares, not RANSAC, because every correspondence is already id-matched against surveyed ground truth, not putative feature matching, so there are no outliers to robustly reject, only a well- or ill-conditioned system to solve exactly. Returns `None` (never raises) on a non-finite result or a determinant below `_MIN_HOMOGRAPHY_DETERMINANT` (1e-12).
11. `_reprojection_error_cm` computes RMS distance between each surveyed world position and where the solved homography reprojects the (possibly undistorted) matched pixel. Exceeding `max_reprojection_error_cm` fails the attempt with the measured value still reported.
12. On success, a `Calibration` is constructed and stored under the lock; `CalibrationResult(succeeded=True, ...)` is returned with `matched_ids`, `reprojection_error_cm`, and the intrinsics-reporting fields.

**Intrinsics estimation** (`_estimate_intrinsics`, sprint 030 ticket 002) fits one camera's lens model from a single view's surveyed-and-detected points via `cv2.calibrateCamera`. Mode selection is graded and decided from what was actually surveyed/detected this burst — never a per-call argument:
- **Coplanar-constrained** (default): `CALIB_FIX_PRINCIPAL_POINT | CALIB_FIX_ASPECT_RATIO | CALIB_ZERO_TANGENT_DIST | CALIB_FIX_K3`, plus `CALIB_FIX_K2` unless `_FREE_K2_IN_COPLANAR_DEFAULT` (fixed `False`). No initial camera-matrix guess is passed when every fit point is genuinely `z=0` — deliberately, since OpenCV's self-initialization is what lets the sanity check below actually fire on a degenerate view; seeding an explicit guess would let a degenerate fit converge toward the guess and mask the degeneracy.
- **Elevated** (freed further): plain `CALIB_USE_INTRINSIC_GUESS` with a generic seed — every intrinsic parameter is free, since genuine 3D structure resolves the ambiguity the constrained flags exist to defend against.
- The switch is `elevated_feature_count >= MIN_ELEVATED_FEATURES_FOR_ELEVATED_MODE (3) and z_spread_cm >= MIN_ELEVATED_Z_SPREAD_CM (5.0)` — both floors chosen with real margin above the ticket's own empirical convergence boundary (3 cm).
- Held-out validation (`_held_out_split`, evenly spaced via `numpy.linspace`, ~70/30) reuses the single solved extrinsic pose to project the held-out points and reports `held_out_rms_px`, informational only — the actual gate is `_sanity_check_intrinsics`, which independently bounds the fitted focal length against image width (`_MIN/MAX_FX_TO_WIDTH_RATIO = 0.3`/`3.0`) and the distortion coefficients themselves (`|k1|`/`|k2| ≤ 1.5`, `|k2/k1| ≤ 3.0` when k2 is free). A rejection here rejects the whole `calibrate()` call.

**Two independent degeneracies, not one.** The module docstring documents both explicitly: (1) v1's classic problem — a single coplanar view underconstrains a fully-free `cv2.calibrateCamera` fit (v1's stored `dist_coeffs [0.42, -2.43, ...]` is the concrete artifact the k1/k2 bounds are tuned against); (2) a newly-discovered, independent ambiguity specific to a near-exactly-nadir mount, present *even under the constrained flag set*: for a straight-down camera, a surveyed point's image position depends on focal length and camera-to-plane distance only through their ratio, so `fx` and the solved extrinsic `tvec[2]` trade off along a flat ridge with no distortion coefficient involved at all. Neither is fixable by choosing different flags — both are structural properties of what a single view contains — so both are caught by rejecting the *fitted coefficients themselves*, never by a pre-fit judgement.

**Decision: home for flat-field capture math (sprint 037).** *Context:* the new math needs `cv2` (median/Gaussian blur), reuses `Deskewer`'s world-corner math and `TagDetector` for mask derivation, and produces a measured artifact persisted next to `calibration.json` — but is radiometric, not pixel↔world, work. *Alternatives considered:* (1) a brand-new top-level subsystem (`src/aprilcam/flatfield/`) — rejected as disproportionate for one new module, and it would need a from-scratch `DESIGN.md` with no canonical predecessor to seed an overlay from, versus extending an already-adjacent package; (2) `daemon/` directly, alongside `detection.py` — rejected because `DetectionEngine` is orchestration glue that needs direct `Camera`/`vision` access, while this math (like `Calibrator`) is cleanly injectable and camera-agnostic, and putting cv2 math directly in `daemon/service.py` would violate that file's own "no business logic, only orchestration" rule (`daemon/DESIGN.md` §1); (3) `camera/` — rejected outright: `camera/` is deliberately cv2-free today (`Camera._apply_flatfield` is pure NumPy) and adding cv2 there would pull OpenCV into every `Camera` consumer, not just the capture path. *Why this choice:* `calibration/` already owns "measured, camera-mounting-tied artifacts solved from a captured frame burst, injected frame source, no camera ownership" — `FlatFieldCapturer` fits that shape structurally even though its output isn't geometric, and it gets `Deskewer`/`geometry`/`TagDetector` reuse for free. *Consequences:* this package's one-sentence purpose test (§1) no longer holds literally for the whole package — accepted and named explicitly (see §1) rather than either stretching the sentence to cover both concerns awkwardly or forcing a heavier structural split for one module.

**Rejected alternatives recorded in the code:**
- v1's `homocal` CLI and its upper-left-origin corner table are explicitly not carried forward (`specification.md` §6 names this a foot-gun). Matching detected ids directly against `PlayfieldDefinition.aruco_position` supersedes v1's `_assign_corners_by_position` canonical id tables entirely, and is what structurally eliminates the origin-shift hazard (Constraints, above) rather than merely avoiding it by careful arithmetic.
- RANSAC for the homography solve was rejected in favor of plain least squares — there are no outliers in an id-matched correspondence set.
- A trailing-block held-out split was the first draft for `_held_out_split`, rejected once testing showed it could hold out *every* elevated point at once (this codebase conventionally appends elevated features last), starving the fit subset of the very 3D structure elevated mode needs. Evenly-spaced indices make that exclusion structurally impossible.
- Freeing `k2` in the coplanar-constrained default (Open Question 5) was tried and rejected: 200 synthetic trials at a well-conditioned 8° tilt showed freeing it raised mean held-out RMS 4-6x, only measurably better in 18-28% of trials.
- For the camera-geometry solver (`solve_camera_geometry`), intersecting the two displacement lines (surveyed->observed, extended) was tried first and produced a nadir ~20 cm off with 150x worse residual than optimum — camera nadir, a reference's true footprint, and its apparent position are collinear by construction under parallax, so two references on opposite sides of the nadir produce nearly-coincident lines, using only direction and discarding magnitude. Full 2D least squares over the same data (via a bilinear->linear substitution, `A = C/H`, `b = 1/H`) is well-conditioned on the identical input.
- **A confirmed bug, fixed via `get_prior_calibration`.** Before this parameter existed, ordinary (non-estimating) recalibration read `Calibrator`'s own in-process cache to carry a lens model forward — correct for same-process recalibration, but empty on a freshly restarted daemon that had loaded real intrinsics from disk but not yet run an in-process `calibrate()`. The result: recalibrating immediately after a daemon restart silently wiped a previously-estimated lens model back to `None`. `daemon.service.DaemonService.get_calibration` (its disk-backed store) is now injected as `get_prior_calibration`, closing the gap.
- **Splitting `undistort_point`/`distort_point` into `distortion.py` instead of leaving them in `geometry.py` (sprint 036, Decision).** *Context:* `client/renderer.py` and `cli/locate.py` import `geometry.py` for genuinely `cv2`-free functions (`world_to_deskewed_pixel`, `solve_camera_geometry`), but `geometry.py`'s own top-level `import cv2 as cv` — needed only by the two distortion functions — made both of those importers transitively `cv2`-dependent, defeating the base-install cv2-free promise the moment `opencv-contrib-python` stopped being an unconditional base dependency (sprint 036's packaging-boundary fix, tracked in the same sprint). *Alternatives considered:* (1) leave `geometry.py` as one file and lazy-import `cv2` inside just the two distortion functions — rejected because a lazy import inside a module two outside packages treat as their stable, always-safe entry point is a subtler hazard than a file boundary: a future function added to `geometry.py` could reintroduce a top-level `cv2` import without anyone noticing it broke `client/renderer.py`. (2) Have `client/renderer.py`/`cli/locate.py` vendor or reimplement `world_to_deskewed_pixel`/`solve_camera_geometry` to avoid importing `geometry.py` at all — rejected, it would duplicate sacrosanct sign-convention math (`design.md` §6) into two more places, exactly what `geometry.py`'s single-source-of-truth docstring exists to prevent. *Why this choice:* a plain module split makes "does importing this module require `cv2`" a structural fact checkable by reading `geometry.py`'s own imports, not a behavioral property of which functions happen to get called. *Consequences:* `calibration.py` now imports two sibling modules (`geometry`, `distortion`) instead of one; `tests/aprilcam/calibration/test_geometry.py`'s `undistort_point`/`distort_point` tests move to a new `test_distortion.py` alongside the code they test.

**The flat-field capture algorithm, end to end** (`FlatFieldCapturer.build`, sprint 037):
1. Given a frame burst (already captured, at whatever temporary low-exposure override `daemon/service.py` pushed) and a boolean field-interior mask, normalise each frame to the batch mean brightness — corrects for the ~25% frame-to-frame light flicker measured at short exposures without needing a flicker model, since the burst's own mean is the reference.
2. Average the normalised frames.
3. Median-blur (kills sheet wrinkles and specular highlights) each channel independently.
4. Fill outside the mask with the mask's own interior mean (no border bleed into the estimate).
5. Wide Gaussian blur to keep only the low-frequency light field `L` — the same "field mean maps to the same grey in every channel" step that removes both illumination falloff and colour cast together.
6. `gain = target / max(L, eps)` per channel, where `target` is the mean of `L` inside the mask across channels; clip to a fixed sane range (mirrors `Camera._apply_flatfield`'s multiplicative-correction assumption).
7. Quality gate (`_evaluate_quality`): checks in-field clipping (any source frame saturating inside the mask), implausible spread (p98/p2 luma ratio inside the mask exceeding a fixed threshold), and frame-to-frame disagreement (each frame's own low-frequency light field, computed independently, deviating from the batch's beyond the flicker-normalisation tolerance already applied in step 1 — this catches the sheet *moving*, which per-frame mean normalisation alone cannot). Any check failing without `force=True` returns `FlatFieldResult(succeeded=False, reason=...)` with the *measured* spread still reported (mirrors `CalibrationResult.reprojection_error_cm`'s "report the number even on a threshold rejection" pattern) — never a silently-passed gate.
8. On success (or `force=True`), returns `FlatFieldResult(succeeded=True, spread_before=..., spread_after=..., gain_min=..., gain_max=..., forced_checks=...)` carrying the built gain array — `daemon/service.py` is what persists it.

**Field-interior mask derivation** is `daemon/service.py`'s job (not `flatfield.py`'s — see the "never opens a camera" invariant above), via one of two paths: (a) **calibrated** — the playfield's four world corners (`+-width_cm/2`, `+-height_cm/2`, reusing `deskew.py`'s `_world_corners`) converted through the camera's stored `Calibration.to_pixel`, filled as a polygon; (b) **uncalibrated** — the linked playfield's required ArUco corner ids, detected directly in one captured frame via a `vision.detector.TagDetector`, convex-hulled into a polygon. Neither calibrated nor a detectable ArUco set: `daemon/service.py` raises `NoFieldMask` before any capture burst is taken — the same "precondition fails before hardware access" discipline `NoPlayfieldLinked` already gets from `Calibrator`.

**Deskew** (`Deskewer`) is architecturally separate from the homography math: it is constructed once per camera+playfield from an already-solved `Calibration` and the playfield's `width_cm`/`height_cm`, computes `output_size`/`px_per_cm`/`world_extent` once at construction (stable for the instance's life, never recomputed per frame), and warps via two independently-built quads sharing the same four world corners — source in raw camera pixel space (`Calibration.to_pixel`), destination the literal output-canvas corners (deliberately *not* `world_to_deskewed_pixel`'s own output, which can fall a sub-pixel short of the canvas edge on a non-square field). `px_per_cm` is always derived from the actual output width, never a chosen constant (`specification.md` §4.1).

### Lens store and carry-forward (sprint 044)

`lens.py` (`cv2`-free) holds `LensModel` (camera matrix, distortion coefficients, image size, source) and `LensStore`, which persists one model per camera at `<state_dir>/cameras/<slug>/intrinsics.json` (atomic tmp+fsync+rename; a corrupt file reads as "no model" and is tolerated, never raised). Measured models are produced by the `intrinsics/` workflow (ChArUco board); they are installed here and nowhere else.

**Carry-forward order.** When `Calibrator.calibrate()` needs a lens model it takes, in order: (1) the store's model if its image size matches the frame; (2) the prior `calibration.json`'s embedded matrix, read as source `legacy` when the file has no `lens_source`; (3) none. A field calibration therefore never discards a measured lens. `Calibration.lens_source` is persisted; `CalibrationResult.lens_model_source` reports which was used and `lens_warning` flags a resolution mismatch (a store model of the wrong size is not applied).

**Decision 2 pairing.** `--with-intrinsics` (estimating a lens from the field markers) is refused while a measured model is installed, because the two sources would fight over the same slot; it remains the documented fallback when no board is available. The six-point estimate crash is now a clean rejection.

## 5. Interfaces

### Exposes

- **`Calibrator.calibrate(camera_slug: str, *, estimate_intrinsics: bool = False) -> CalibrationResult`** — the concrete implementation of `interfaces.Calibrator.calibrate`. Preconditions: raises `NoPlayfieldLinked` if the camera has no config or no playfield link (or the daemon holds no definition by that name); raises `TypeError` for a non-concrete `PlayfieldDefinition`; raises `RuntimeError` if `capture_frames` returns zero frames. Never raises for a failed detection/solve — those are reported through `CalibrationResult.succeeded=False` with `reason`. A failed attempt never mutates any previously-stored good `Calibration` for that camera. Thread-safe (internal lock).
- **`Calibrator.get_calibration(camera_slug: str) -> Optional[Calibration]`** — additive, not on the base interface (mirrors `PlayfieldDefinition.aruco_position`'s "additive helper on the concrete class" pattern). The most recent successful calibration this instance produced, or `None`.
- **`Calibration.to_world(pixel: PixelPoint) -> WorldPoint`** — undistorts first (via `geometry.undistort_point`) if `camera_matrix`/`dist_coeffs` are both set, then maps through the stored homography (`geometry.pixel_to_world`). Pure, no I/O; A1-centred, +x east, +y north, per the sacrosanct convention.
- **`Calibration.to_pixel(world: WorldPoint) -> PixelPoint`** — inverse: maps through the homography's inverse, then redistorts if intrinsics are known (bounded fixed-point iteration in `geometry.distort_point`, up to 200 iterations / 1e-6 px tolerance — not globally convergent for pathological coefficients, though the sanity-check gate on what gets stored bounds how pathological a real stored model can be).
- **`Calibration.to_dict()` / `Calibration.from_dict(data)`** — the `calibration.json` serialization shape (owned mechanically here; the atomic write itself is `daemon/service.py`'s job). `camera_matrix`/`dist_coeffs` are omitted entirely (never written as JSON `null`) when unknown, and default to `None` on load — every `calibration.json` written before lens-distortion support loads unchanged.
- **`compute_drift_cm(calibration, definition, detected) -> Optional[float]`** — module-level function, the opportunistic drift check: re-projects a later frame's re-detection of `calibration`'s own matched markers through the still-valid homography and compares to `definition`'s surveyed positions, returning the **maximum** single-marker error (not RMS). Returns `None` — never `0.0` — below `MIN_DRIFT_CHECK_MARKERS` (3) visible matched markers.
- **`Deskewer(calibration, width_cm, height_cm, max_dimension_px=800)`** with `.deskew(frame: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray` and read-only `output_size`/`px_per_cm`/`world_extent` properties — the concrete `interfaces.Deskewer`.
- **`FlatFieldCapturer.build(frames: Sequence[np.ndarray], mask: np.ndarray, *, force: bool = False) -> FlatFieldResult`** (sprint 037) — the concrete `interfaces.FlatFieldCapturer`. Never raises for a failed quality-gate check (reported via `FlatFieldResult.succeeded=False`/`reason`, mirroring `Calibrator.calibrate`'s own "explicit failure is data" contract); never touches a filesystem or a camera.
- **`FlatFieldResult`** (frozen `@dataclass(kw_only=True)`, sprint 037) — `succeeded`, `spread_before`/`spread_after` (per-channel p98/p2 tuples), `reason`, `forced_checks` (which gate(s) `force=True` overrode, empty when none did), plus the built `gain: Optional[np.ndarray]` (only on success or a forced pass).
- **`FlatFieldInfo`** (frozen `@dataclass(kw_only=True)`, sprint 037) — the `flatfield.json` sidecar shape: `present`, `captured_at`, `shape`, `gain_min`/`gain_max`, `stale`. `cv2`-free (lives in `interfaces.py`, not `flatfield.py`) — the same "value types stay importable by `client/`/`mcp/`" reasoning `Calibration`/`CalibrationResult` already get.
- **`geometry.py`'s pure functions** are all public API in practice: `pixel_to_world`/`world_to_pixel`, `deskewed_output_size`/`world_to_deskewed_pixel`, `project_tag_to_field_plane`/`apply_mount_offset`/`correct_tag_pose`, and `solve_camera_geometry` (with `ReferenceObservation`/`CameraGeometrySolution`) are imported directly by consumers outside this package (`client/renderer.py`, `daemon/detection.py`, `cli/locate.py`) — none of which need or import `distortion.py`.
- **`distortion.py`'s `undistort_point`/`distort_point`** are consumed only by `calibration.py` (`Calibration.to_world`/`to_pixel`, and the homography-solve undistort step) — no outside-package consumer.

### Consumes

All by reference — `calibration/` has no import from `daemon/`; the dependency runs one way (daemon depends on calibration, not vice versa).

- **`vision/detector.py`** — `TagDetector` (constructed via an injectable `detector_factory`, defaulting to a fresh instance), reused across every `calibrate()` call rather than reimplemented. `.detect(gray, scale=...)` returns per-family `Detection`s.
- **`vision/pipeline.py`** — `run_pipeline`/`PipelineConfig`, the same preprocess-then-detect pipeline `daemon/detection.py` uses, so calibration detection and live tag detection see identical preprocessing.
- **`config/interfaces.py`** — `CameraConfig` (for the `.playfield` link) and the abstract `PlayfieldDefinition`/`Feature` shapes.
- **`config/loader.py`** — the concrete `PlayfieldDefinition`; `Calibrator.calibrate` `isinstance`-checks against this specific type and raises `TypeError` otherwise (a defensive check mirrored identically in `daemon/rpc.py` and `client/daemon_client.py`). Reads `.aruco_tags`/`.april_tags`/`.aruco_position()`/`.required_aruco_ids()`.
- **`errors.py`** — `NoPlayfieldLinked`.
- **`types.py`** — `PixelPoint`, `WorldPoint`, `TagFamily`, `MountParameters`.
- **The daemon (by injection, not import)** — `daemon/service.py` is the sole constructor of `Calibrator`/`Deskewer`, binding `get_camera_config`/`get_playfield`/`capture_frames` to its own state, and the sole persister of `Calibration` (writes `calibration.json` before `config.json`, per the global ordering convention; reloads at startup). `daemon/detection.py` separately imports `compute_drift_cm` (opportunistic drift check) and `correct_tag_pose` (per-frame mount/parallax correction) directly, plus `Calibration.to_world` for tag world positions.

## 6. Open Questions / Known Limitations

- **A pre-sprint-037 `flatfield.npy` (built by the manual recipe, `docs/knowledge/flat-field-gain-map.md`) has no `flatfield.json` sidecar.** `get_flatfield` for such a camera reports `present=True` with `captured_at=None`, `stale=False` (never a fabricated timestamp) until the next capture through this workflow writes both files together — a one-time, self-resolving migration gap, not an ongoing one.
- **The >10x implausible-spread threshold and the frame-to-frame disagreement tolerance are fixed constants**, sized against this project's own two real cameras (measured 2.65x/1.32x spread — `docs/knowledge/flat-field-gain-map.md`) and the ~25% flicker their short-exposure captures exhibit, the same "confirmed default, not yet validated against other hardware" caveat `MAX_REPROJECTION_ERROR_CM` carries above.
- **Two intrinsics-estimation questions the code itself once flagged as "Open Question 5" and "Open Question 6" are now resolved by fixed constants**, not left open: whether to free `k2` in the coplanar-constrained default (resolved: no, `_FREE_K2_IN_COPLANAR_DEFAULT = False`) and the elevated-mode threshold (resolved: `MIN_ELEVATED_FEATURES_FOR_ELEVATED_MODE = 3`, `MIN_ELEVATED_Z_SPREAD_CM = 5.0`). Both are backed by empirical investigation the module docstring describes in detail, but that evidence is explicitly scoped to this project's own point-count regime (`main-playfield`'s 11 surveyed points, an 8/3-ish fit/held-out split) — the docstring itself notes this is "not a property any one camera could override its way out of," meaning a playfield with a substantially different point count has not been validated against these specific thresholds.
- **Elevated-mode intrinsics estimation is a comparatively rare, less-exercised path** — it requires ≥3 elevated features *and* ≥5 cm of z-spread simultaneously, and the module docstring flags a specific edge case (a sub-threshold elevated point landing in the held-out split's fit subset) as "rare in practice."
- **`distort_point`'s fixed-point iteration is not globally convergent** — it is a Picard iteration valid for ordinary radial/tangential lens distortion, and is not guaranteed to converge for pathological distortion coefficients (though the intrinsics sanity check bounds how pathological a coefficient set stored by this codebase can ever be, this function itself trusts whatever `dist_coeffs` it's handed).
- **Resolved, sprint 036: the base-package cv2-free promise is now real for this subsystem's math, not merely accidental.** Previously `geometry.py` had a top-level `import cv2 as cv` (needed only by `undistort_point`/`distort_point`), and `client/renderer.py` — one of the packages `tests/test_no_cv2_in_client_modules.py` mechanically enforces as cv2-free at the AST level — imported `world_to_deskewed_pixel` directly from that same module, so it worked only because `opencv-contrib-python` happened to be a base dependency for an unrelated reason (`tags/`'s taggen). Splitting the two `cv2`-dependent functions into `distortion.py` (see §2, Design Rationale) means `geometry.py` now has zero `cv2` dependency of its own, so `client/renderer.py`'s and `cli/locate.py`'s imports of it no longer transitively require `cv2` at all — this holds regardless of which `pip install` extra `opencv-contrib-python` lives in, which is what makes it a real fix rather than a coincidence of the old packaging.
- **A camera with no `camera_position` configured falls back to a `(0, 0)`/height-`0.0` sentinel for parallax correction** — documented in `geometry.py` as "nothing to correct," a deliberate stopgap rather than an error, meaning a camera that has never been located reports elevated-tag world positions without parallax correction rather than refusing to report them.
